# Focus — Vision

## Vision

Focus is a family of products designed to scale the knowledge of software systems.

Its purpose is to transform information from reliable sources and validated context into structured, accessible, traceable, and continuously evolving knowledge about a software platform.

Focus seeks to make that knowledge useful to both technical and non-technical people, enabling organizations to better understand their systems, communicate their knowledge, onboard new resources, maintain documentation, identify risks and vulnerabilities, and progressively build new capabilities on top of the same knowledge foundation.

Documentation is the initial product and the first way in which this knowledge becomes directly useful to users. However, the long-term vision is not limited to documentation. The knowledge model is the foundation upon which the Focus product family can evolve.

## Core Principles

### 1. Knowledge must be grounded in evidence

Focus should prioritize information obtained from reliable and direct sources, including software repositories, snapshots, existing documentation, structured data, and validated human context.

Information should not be treated as factual merely because it can be inferred.

### 2. Traceability is fundamental

Every relevant piece of knowledge should be traceable to its source or supporting evidence.

Focus should make it possible to understand why a piece of information exists in the knowledge model and where it originated.

### 3. Inference should be minimized

Inference can be useful when direct information is unavailable, but it should never silently become fact.

When information is inferred, Focus should distinguish it from directly observed or explicitly provided information.

### 4. Human validation is mandatory for relevant knowledge

Knowledge generated or inferred by Focus must be subject to validation and approval by the appropriate people within the organization.

Focus supports and scales human knowledge; it does not replace the people responsible for validating the meaning and correctness of that knowledge.

### 5. Knowledge should evolve with the system

Software changes continuously. Therefore, the knowledge describing it must also be capable of evolving.

Focus should progressively connect changes, sources, context, and validated knowledge so that the resulting representation remains useful as the system changes.

### 6. One knowledge foundation, multiple capabilities

The knowledge model should serve as a common foundation for different products and experiences.

Potential capabilities include:

- Automated documentation
- Knowledge and onboarding interfaces
- System and architecture visualization
- Process and flow analysis
- Risk analysis
- Security and vulnerability analysis
- Impact analysis
- AI-assisted access to organizational knowledge

These capabilities are not independent products disconnected from one another. They are different ways of using the same underlying knowledge.

## What Focus Is

Focus is a system for building, maintaining, and exposing structured knowledge about software systems and their surrounding context.

It combines information from software sources with contextual and human knowledge, while preserving the relationship between knowledge, evidence, and validation.

## What Focus Is Not

Focus is not intended to:

- Modify the customer's software or source code without explicit authorization.
- Invent business rules, system behavior, or context without supporting evidence.
- Present unvalidated inference as established fact.
- Replace the people responsible for understanding and validating the organization's systems.
- Limit itself to generating static documentation.

## Long-Term Direction

The long-term goal of Focus is to become a trusted knowledge layer for software organizations: a continuously evolving representation of how their systems work, why they work that way, how their components relate to one another, and what context surrounds them.

Documentation is the initial entry point.

The broader vision is to make the same trusted knowledge available wherever an organization needs to understand, communicate, analyze, or manage its software systems.
